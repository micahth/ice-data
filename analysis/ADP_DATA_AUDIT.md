# Stage 1: detention interval audit

Source: `/workspaces/ice-data/micahth/ice-data/analysis/results/detention_interval_audit.csv`, generated by
`/workspaces/ice-data/micahth/ice-data/analysis/audit_detention_intervals.py`
(deduplicated stints, cutoff 2026-03-11, monthly half-open windows). Nothing in `ice_lib.build_intervals()`
or any published result was changed. ADP is not a unique-person count.

## Transfer double counting is small

- 5,594 stints overlap the next stint in their stay, totalling about 874 person-days over the whole record.
- Only one stint ends via the stay-end fallback, so the planned concern about missing stint book-outs is
  almost entirely absent. Most overlaps come from stint book-out times that run past the next book-in.
- Clipping each stint at the next stint's book-in lowers monthly ADP by at most 0.0097% (March 2026,
  partial month); for 2025-01 to 2026-02 the largest reduction is 0.0072%.
- Some stints belong to a person with another overlapping stay (`concurrent_stay_stints`); this is
  flagged but not removed.

## Open-stint policy

- 1,049 stints in total are open and excluded (booked in before 2025-01-01, no end). This peaks at 416
  book-ins in December 2024 and is negligible next to monthly ADP of about 37,000.
- 60,582 stints are censored at the cutoff. Censored book-ins climb from 657 (2025-01) to 16,510
  (2026-02), so recent ADP depends increasingly on the censoring assumption.
- No stints have a negative interval or a missing book-in.

## First trustworthy month for Series A

The extract has isolated book-ins from 2004 to mid-2022, then ramps: 1,203 book-ins in 2022-06, 13,127 in
2022-09 and 37,149 in 2022-10. Earlier months lack most stays, so ADP there is left-truncated, not real.
Book-in volume is stable from 2022-10. I recommend **2023-01** as the first trustworthy month: it gives
roughly three months of full intake, and the share of person-days from stints booked more than 90 days
earlier is 0.13 and settles near 0.10 by 2023-03. The share booked more than 365 days earlier stays
near 0.01, so long-stay burn-in is minor from late 2022. Treat 2022-10 to 2022-12 as sensitivity only.

## Open questions

- Is ADP of about 24,000 to 30,000 in 2022-12 to 2023-06 low relative to ICE's published ADP? This is a
  Stage 2 reconciliation question.
- The 2022 ramp implies the extract is defined by a window that may drop stays booked in earlier. Check
  against the published series before using months before 2023-01.

## Stage 2: published series (Series B)

- Source: DDP `ice-detention-management` repo, `adp-and-stay-length-by-agency-monthly` (pinned to commit
  `5126c6e8`, SHA-256 checked). Total/total monthly ADP, 94 contiguous months, 2018-10 to 2026-07. ICE and
  CBP components sum to the total. The monthly table has one vintage per month, so revisions are only
  visible in the FYTD table (`published_adp_revisions.csv`).
- The day-weighted monthly mean matches the latest published FYTD figure within 1% in 7 of 8 fiscal
  years; FY2021 differs by 1.1% (published FYTD was revised).
- Reconciliation with Series A (`adp_current_method`): Series A is far below Series B before 2022-10
  (left truncation) and 0.95 to 0.98 of it from 2022-10 on. This supports 2022-10 as the start of usable
  record-level data; the 2023-01 recommendation above stays the conservative choice. Series A has no
  stated reason for the persistent 2 to 5% gap (scope or definition); it is not adjusted away.

## Stage 3: transfer-safe target and reconciliation

Generated by `analysis/07_monthly_adp.py` into `analysis/results/monthly_adp_records.csv` (transfer-safe
`adp` plus `adp_current_method`, `adp_censor_all`, `adp_exclude_all`; the censoring variants also use the
transfer-safe rule) and `analysis/results/adp_series_reconciliation.csv`. Reconciliation covers the 38
complete months 2023-01 to 2026-02.

- Series A (records) is below Series B (published, latest vintage) in every month: mean difference
  -1,550 and median -935 ADP (range -3,579 to -523), or a mean of -3.29% (median -2.75%).
- The gap is not stable. It is about -2% through mid-2024, widens to about -4.5% by 2025-01 and sits near
  -5% (ratio 0.950) from 2025-05 on. The trend over the period is -0.10 percentage points per month.
- Month-over-month changes correlate at 0.998, so the series move together; they differ mainly in level.
- The monthly table has one vintage per month, so "first" and "latest" published values are identical
  here; real-time revisions are not measurable at the monthly level.

Likely definitional reasons (not tested here): the published figures may count hold rooms and very short
stays, may cover facilities absent from the stint extract, and are snapshot-based while Series A is
interval-based; the open-stint policy and later revisions also matter. The near-constant 0.950 ratio in
late months suggests a systematic scope or definition difference rather than noise, but this is not
established.

**Modeling target decision:** Series B (published, latest vintage) is the primary target because it has
94 months against 38. Series A is the secondary, validation series over 2023-01 to 2026-02. The levels
differ and the gap drifts, so the series are modeled separately and never spliced; any level-adjusted
combination may only be a labeled sensitivity check with a `source` column.

## Stage 4: baselines

Generated by `analysis/forecast_adp.py` (baseline rows in `analysis/results/adp_forecast_backtest.csv`). Last-month is the
baseline to beat: on the published series its mean absolute error is 1,951 ADP (57 forecasts, 2021-10 to
2026-06); on the record series it is 2,479 (14 forecasts, 2025-01 to 2026-02). Seasonal-naive is much
worse because ADP trends rather than cycling. The seasonal-naive-drift baseline is an extra.

**Note on July 2026:** the latest published file was pulled on 2026-07-11, so its July 2026 value
(65,634) does not cover the whole month. It is treated as partial and excluded from backtests and
projections; the last complete published month is 2026-06. The September 2025 value was also pulled
before month-end (2025-09-20) and is used as published, so it may be slightly short.

## Stage 5: models

Model list fixed before running (`MODELS` in `analysis/forecast_adp.py`): `log_ar1`, `log_ar1_seasonal`
(only with at least 36 training months), `ets_damped` and, for the record series only,
`log_ar1_arrests` (arrests at t-1 only), plus the three baselines. Outputs:
`analysis/results/adp_forecast_backtest.csv` (with `source` and `fit_status` on every row) and
`adp_forecast_projection.csv`. No hyperparameters were tuned. All fits succeeded where applicable.

Mean absolute error, one month ahead (full samples, not split by regime yet; that is Stage 6):

| Model | Published (57 forecasts) | Records (14 forecasts) |
|---|---|---|
| last_month | 1,951 | 2,479 |
| log_ar1 | 2,081 | 2,736 |
| ets_damped | 2,024 | 2,224 |
| log_ar1_arrests | n/a | 2,488 |
| log_ar1_seasonal | 2,386 | 5,289 (2 forecasts only) |

On the published series no model beats last_month on MAE. On the short record series `ets_damped` is
lower (2,224 vs 2,479), but with 14 forecasts this is weak evidence. Adding lagged arrests did not help
over `log_ar1` by much (2,488 vs 2,736) and does not beat last_month. The projections for the next
complete month (published 2026-07, records 2026-03) are in `adp_forecast_projection.csv`; they are
descriptive, not a claim of deployment readiness.

## Stage 6: evaluation and intervals

`analysis/results/adp_forecast_metrics.csv` has one row per source, model and period (full, pre_covid,
covid, 2022_2024, 2025_on); `adp_forecast_backtest.csv` now carries 80% and 95% bounds. Intervals are
empirical quantiles of earlier one-step log errors, with at least 12 prior errors (published) or 6 (records,
which has only 14 forecasts). Diebold-Mariano tests against `last_month` are in
`adp_forecast_dm_tests.csv` (absolute and squared error; Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold correction; two-sided
p-values; at least 8 paired forecasts; no multiplicity adjustment). The published backtest starts in 2021-10, so `pre_covid` has no forecasts and `covid`
has only 3.

Models with lower MAE than `last_month` (descriptive; few, serially dependent origins):

| Source | Period | Forecasts | Lower MAE than last_month |
|---|---|---|---|
| published | full | 57 | none |
| published | covid | 3 | log_ar1 |
| published | 2022_2024 | 36 | none |
| published | 2025_on | 18 | ets_damped (MAE 2,232 vs 2,723; MASE 0.82) |
| records | full / 2025_on | 14 | ets_damped (MASE 0.90) |

- Bias: every model under-forecasts on average in the published series because ADP rose over the sample
  (last_month mean error -637, and -1,127 from 2025). `ets_damped` has the smallest bias (-129 overall).
- Coverage, published full sample (about 45 forecasts with intervals): last_month 80% interval covers
  0.80 and 95% covers 0.956; mean 95% width is about 13,300 ADP. 2025 onward has
  coverage of 0.89 and 1.00 for last_month, but only 18 origins.
- Records: 8 of 14 forecasts have intervals (6 prior errors are required); last_month covers 0.75 and 0.75.
  With 8 forecasts this is too noisy to judge calibration.
- Diebold-Mariano (absolute error, vs last_month): the `ets_damped` gain from 2025 on is not significant
  (mean gain 490.1, p = 0.130; records p = 0.315). In 2022-2024 `ets_damped` is significantly worse
  (p = 0.047). `log_ar1_arrests` (records only) is indistinguishable from last_month (p = 0.989).
  `seasonal_naive` is significantly worse everywhere.
- The gain of `ets_damped` appears only in the 2025 policy regime and is not a consistent advantage; no
  model reliably beats last_month. These results are not evidence of deployment readiness.

## Follow-up: why records ADP is below published ADP

Checked from the existing CSVs (`monthly_adp_records.csv`, `ice_published_adp_monthly.csv`); this explains
part of the gap, not all of it.

- **Not an ICE-vs-CBP mix-up.** Records ADP tracks the published *Total* (ICE plus CBP), not the ICE-only
  component: records / Total is 0.95 to 0.98, while records / ICE-only ranges from 1.1 to 3.7.
- **Not transfers.** Stage 1 found transfer double counting is at most 0.0097% of monthly ADP.
- **The gap is not constant.** Records / published is about 0.974 to 0.981 from 2023-01 to mid-2024, falls
  to about 0.955 by 2025-01, and sits at 0.950 to 0.951 from 2025-10 to 2026-02.
- **Open-record handling matters.** Censoring every open stint (`adp_censor_all`) raises the ratio to about
  0.965 to 0.982, which shrinks but does not close the gap. Excluding every open stint widens it.
- **Not tested.** Whether published ADP counts partial days as whole days, and whether it counts stays that
  the extract omits. Both need row-level work on the raw extract that was not run.

Consequence: keep the two series separate. Published stays the primary target, and records is used to
check direction and month-to-month changes (correlation 0.998), not level.
