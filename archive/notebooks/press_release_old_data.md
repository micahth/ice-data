## Nine in ten ICE arrests now end in detention, up from six in ten, as arrests more than double and detention stays shorten

*Analysis of the Deportation Data Project's ICE records (extract dated July 24, 2026; arrests Oct 2022 – Mar 2026)*

**Key findings**

- **Detention became the default.** 61.3% of arrests under the Biden administration (Oct 2022 – Jan 2025) led to a detention stay, versus **90.0%** from Jan 2025 on. Arrests that never led to detention fell from 121,987 to 38,998.
- **Arrests surged.** Full-month arrests averaged **11,532 per month** in the Biden-era window and **28,624 per month** from Feb 2025 through Feb 2026 (peak: 39,829 in December 2025).
- **Per-arrest detention:** counting people who were arrested but never detained as 0 days, the average arrest meant **31.3 days** in detention (median 9.8) versus 41.5 days (median 21.9) for detained people alone. Biden-era: 29.7 days; Trump 2nd: 32.8 days.
- **Who is held:** 93.1% of arrests of people with convictions and 92.6% of those with pending charges were detained, versus 54.1% of "other immigration violators." By nationality, detention ranged from 90.0% (Honduras) to 37.8% (Nicaragua).
- **How people leave:** removal accounted for 55.0% of Biden-era book-outs and **83.8%** under Trump 2nd. Parole fell from 20.7% to 0.5% and orders of recognizance from 12.7% to 1.9%.
- **Stay length:** released stays had a median of **26.7 days** (mean 45.9, n = 1,025,342). Median stays ranged from 8.2 days (Mexico) to 51.7 days (Nicaragua) and from 9.9 days (order of recognizance releases) to 109.9 days (relief or proceedings ended).
- **Current population (as of the data's end, March 2026):** 62,034 stays had no book-out date (5.7%).

**Caveats.** The stays data end at March 11, 2026, about 134 days before the extraction date, so recent stays are right-censored and Trump 2nd stay lengths are biased downward. Bond records exist for only 7.4% of stays. All findings are descriptive and do not establish cause.
