# PRESS RELEASE — Based on the Sept 2026 ICE Data Release

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

## ICE held about 72,000 people at its January 2026 peak, double the 2021–2025 average; new data through August 2026 show arrests still above 30,000 a month

*Analysis of the Deportation Data Project's September 2026 ICE release (arrests and stays through August 6, 2026)*

**Key findings**

- **Arrests kept climbing.** Full-month arrests averaged **11,509 per month** in the Oct 2022 – Jan 2025 window and **30,932 per month** from Feb 2025 through Jul 2026. The record month in the data is **July 2026 with 49,091 arrests**.
- **Detention population doubled.** Reconstructing the daily population from stay book-in/out dates gives a mean of **about 33,500** during the Oct 2022 – Jan 2025 window, **39,845** in January 2025, and a peak of **72,012 on January 15, 2026**. July 2026 averaged 65,563.
- **Detention is the default.** 89.1% of arrests since Jan 2025 led to a detention stay (886,369 arrests overall: 79.3% detained, 91.7% of those booked out, 77.7% of those removed).
- **Average detention per arrest, including never-detained arrests: 32.9 days** (median 11.8), versus 42.2 days among detained people.
- **How arrests happen is changing.** At-large arrests rose from 41.1% to 48.3% of arrests, and 287(g) local-partnership arrests from 2.0% to 8.1%. Custodial arrests fell from 46.7% to 41.9%.
- **Who is arrested.** Mexican citizens rose from 31.2% to 40.4% of arrests and Guatemalans from 8.2% to 14.1%, while Nicaraguans fell from 10.0% to 3.0%. Women fell from 20.4% to 11.5% and people under 18 from 7.8% to 1.6%.
- **Existing final orders.** 28.1% of arrests since Jan 2025 involved someone with a final order before the arrest (23.3% earlier); 45.6% among convicted-criminal arrests.
- **Detainers nearly doubled**: 11,508 to 21,119 per month; Florida's share rose from 6.1% to 12.0%.
- **Today's open stays are recent.** 66,102 stays have no book-out; **44,799 are 90 days old or less**, with a median of 41.6 days in custody.

**Caveats.** "Posted bond" rates cannot be measured in this release (see report). Roughly 11% of detained Trump 2nd arrests lack a book-out date, so recent stay lengths are biased downward. Descriptive findings only.
